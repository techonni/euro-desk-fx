A **trading journal** is a written record of what you did on the chart and why. On EURUSD and GBPUSD, the market moves fast during London and New York hours. Without notes, you forget the real reason you clicked. You only remember the feeling. A journal slows that down.

This guide uses beginner English and simple action verbs. You will learn what to write, how to keep the habit small, and how to review one week of notes without chasing “perfect” trades. This is education only. It is **not financial advice**. Nothing here is a trade order, a signal to click, or a promise of profit.

## Why a journal matters more than another indicator

Many beginners add more tools when results feel messy. A new moving average. A new oscillator. A new color on the candles. The chart gets busy. The habit stays the same.

A journal attacks a different problem: **memory**. After a win, you may tell yourself you “saw it early.” After a loss, you may say the market was “unfair.” Both stories can be wrong. Written notes show what you planned before the candle closed.

On EURUSD and GBPUSD, one pair can look calm while the other spikes. If you trade both without notes, you mix sessions, news minutes, and random clicks into one blur. The journal separates them.

Clear goal of a beginner journal:

- Record the setup you thought you saw
- Record the risk you allowed (in pips or account %)
- Record what you did after entry
- Review patterns later with a calm head

The journal does not make EURUSD go up. It makes **your process** visible.

## What to write before you open a study trade

Write the plan **before** you enter. If you write after the result, you edit the story. Keep it short so you can finish in under two minutes.

Useful pre-trade lines:

1. **Pair and session** — EURUSD or GBPUSD? Asian, London, or New York hours?
2. **Setup name** — one short label you already defined (for example: “pullback to prior high” or “range edge”). If you cannot name it, you may be guessing.
3. **Direction** — buy or sell idea for study only.
4. **Invalidation** — the price level where the idea is wrong.
5. **Risk size** — how many pips to the invalidation, and what that means for lot size on your demo.
6. **News check** — any high-impact event on the economic calendar in the next hour?

You do not need fancy software. A notes app, a spreadsheet, or a paper notebook works. The habit beats the tool.

Example block (demo study only):

- Pair: EURUSD  
- Session: London open  
- Setup: bounce from marked support  
- Invalidation: close below support by X pips  
- Risk: 0.5% of demo equity  
- News: no high-impact print in the next 60 minutes  

If you cannot fill those lines, pause. Waiting is a valid choice.

## What to write while the trade is open

Keep mid-trade notes short. Long essays during London volatility invite emotional clicks.

Write only:

- Did price respect your invalidation idea?
- Did you move the stop for a rule you wrote earlier, or for fear?
- Did you add size without a new plan?

On GBPUSD, spreads and spikes can widen around data. Note if the candle moved so fast that your planned stop felt different from what you expected. That note helps later when you study slippage and session timing. It is not a complaint at the market. It is data about your platform and your timing.

Avoid rewriting the thesis mid-trade into a new story. If the idea is wrong at your invalidation, the journal should say so in plain words.

## What to write after you close

After exit, add facts first, feelings second.

Fact lines:

- Entry price and exit price (or chart screenshot time)
- Result in pips (plus or minus)
- Result in account % (demo)
- Did you follow the written plan? Yes / No / Partial
- Session still the same as planned?

Feeling lines (one sentence each):

- What did you want to do that you did not write in the plan?
- Did FOMO, boredom, or revenge show up?

Be honest. A journal that only celebrates wins teaches little. A journal that only insults you after losses also fails. Aim for clear labels: “skipped invalidation,” “entered early,” “held past plan,” “followed plan and lost.” A planned loss is different from a random click.

## A simple weekly review for EURUSD and GBPUSD

Once a week, read seven days of notes. Do not open a new trade while you review. Sit with the text.

Ask four questions:

1. **Which pair had cleaner notes?** Maybe EURUSD notes are clear and GBPUSD notes are messy. That can mean you understand one pair’s rhythm better right now.
2. **Which session produced most “Partial” or “No” plan scores?** Late New York clicks may cluster. London open may cluster. Treat that as a schedule clue, not a personality attack.
3. **Did news minutes break your rules?** If many losses sit on red-folder events, your study rule may need a hard pause around those prints.
4. **Did winners and losers share the same setup name?** If winners are random labels and losers are also random labels, you do not have a setup yet. You have clicks.

Write one improvement line for next week. Only one. Examples:

- “No new GBPUSD entries in the first 15 minutes after high-impact UK data.”
- “Only enter EURUSD if the setup name is on my short list.”
- “Stop size written before entry, always.”

Small rules compound. A journal that generates ten new rules every Sunday becomes noise again.

## Common beginner mistakes with journals

**Writing novels.** If each entry takes twenty minutes, you will quit. Keep templates.

**Skipping losers.** The painful trades hold the lessons. Log them the same day.

**Changing the story after the close.** If you planned a breakout and you actually chased, write “chase.” Accuracy beats ego.

**Tracking only money.** Pips and % matter, but process scores matter more at the start. A week of followed plans with small losses can be healthier study than a week of random wins.

**Mixing live emotions with demo notes.** If you practice on demo for EURUSD and GBPUSD, keep the tone educational. Demo wins do not mean you are ready for larger risk. Demo losses do not mean you are “bad.” Both are practice data.

**Ignoring correlation.** EURUSD and GBPUSD often move with related themes. If you hold study ideas on both at once, note that in the journal. Two “small” risks can feel like one larger risk when both pairs move together.

## How to keep the habit when you feel busy

Link journaling to a trigger you already do:

- After you close the chart for the day, fill the after-trade block
- After London lunch, review morning notes for two minutes
- Every Sunday morning, run the four review questions

If you missed a day, do not write a fake history. Mark “no notes” and continue. Shame spirals kill journals faster than blank pages.

You can also journal **non-trades**. Write when you almost clicked and did not. That line is gold. It shows patience. On busy EURUSD mornings, “I waited” is a result worth recording.

## What a journal is not

A journal is not a magic win rate booster.  
A journal is not financial advice.  
A journal is not a public brag feed.  
A journal is not a replacement for risk limits, stop-loss study, or session awareness.

Use it as a mirror. EURUSD and GBPUSD will keep moving whether you write or not. Your job as a learner is to see your own pattern clearly enough to change one behavior at a time.

## Quick starter template you can copy

Use this block for each study idea:

```
Date:
Pair: EURUSD / GBPUSD
Session:
Setup name:
Plan (entry idea + invalidation + risk %):
News nearby? Y/N
Followed plan? Yes / No / Partial
Result (pips / %):
One lesson:
```

Fill it for ten study trades. Then review. You will usually see repeats: early entries, late exits, news minutes, or unclear setup names. Those repeats are your next lesson list.
