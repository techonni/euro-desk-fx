A **break and retest** is a simple chart story: price moves through a level you care about, then comes back to **touch that same zone again** before continuing (or failing). On EURUSD and GBPUSD, beginners hear this phrase a lot — and then mix it up with a false breakout or a random bounce.

This guide stays in beginner English. You will learn a plain definition, how a retest differs from a false break, how to mark the level before the break, how sessions and news change the look, how to journal retests without guessing the next candle, and soft study rules that keep risk education calm. This is education only. It is **not financial advice**. Nothing here is a trade order or a promise of profit.

## Why break and retest matters after support, resistance, and false breaks

If you already studied **support and resistance**, you know price often pauses or turns near prior swing zones and round numbers. If you studied **false breakouts**, you know a probe beyond a level can fail and snap back.

The next beginner question is quieter:

- Did price **break and hold** beyond the level?
- Did it come **back to retest** that zone before continuing?
- Or was the whole move a **false break** that never held?

Wrong word, wrong notebook lesson. A failed probe studied as a “clean retest setup” makes you rewrite history every hour. A real hold studied as “noise” makes you ignore useful structure. Name the sample. Then write what would force a rewrite. Always **NFA**.

## Break in plain words

A **break** (for beginner study) means price moves **beyond** a level you already marked — a swing high, swing low, range edge, or round number — under a rule you wrote **before** the move.

Soft study language:

- On a resistance break study: candles close (or hold) **above** the zone under your rule.
- On a support break study: candles close (or hold) **below** the zone under your rule.

A single wick that spikes beyond the level and dies is often **not** a break under beginner hold rules. Soft study: decide whether you need a close, two closes, or a hold for N minutes. Write the rule once. Reuse it.

**Soft study rule:** mark the zone and the hold rule **before** price probes it — not after you like the candle color.

### Tiny EURUSD example (illustrative only)

On H1 EURUSD during London, you marked resistance near **1.0900** from prior highs. Price pushes from **1.0875** through **1.0900** and the next H1 candle **closes above** 1.0900 under your rule. In your notebook this is a **break sample** of that zone — not a live order. Numbers are teaching props, not forecasts.

### Tiny GBPUSD example (illustrative only)

On GBPUSD H1, you marked support near **1.2700**. Price slides from **1.2730** through **1.2700** and holds below for two H1 closes. That is a **support break sample** under your written rule. Educational structure only.

## Retest in plain words

A **retest** is when, **after** a break that held under your rule, price comes **back toward the same zone** and pauses, rejects, or fails there.

Soft study language:

- After a resistance break higher: price dips back toward the old resistance (now studied as possible support).
- After a support break lower: price bounces back toward the old support (now studied as possible resistance).

The retest does **not** guarantee continuation. It is a **second look** at the same map. Soft study: write whether the retest **touches**, **enters**, or **overshoots** the zone, and whether your hold rule still agrees with the break.

**Soft study rule:** do not call “retest” until the break already passed your hold filter. A bounce at a level that never broke is just a bounce at S/R — different notebook line.

## Break and retest vs false break (keep the words separate)

| Word | Plain meaning in study notes |
|------|------------------------------|
| **Break** | Price moves beyond a marked level and **holds** under your rule |
| **Retest** | After a held break, price returns to the zone for a second look |
| **False break** | Probe beyond a level that **fails** your hold rule and returns |

A deep spike beyond 1.0900 that closes back under the zone is often a **false break** sample — not a break-and-retest. A clean close above, then a calm dip to 1.0900 that holds, is a classic **break and retest** study sketch. Feelings are optional. Rules are not.

Beginners often say “it retested” when price never broke. Write the break first. Then write the return.

## A simple labeling checklist (one timeframe)

Use this soft checklist on H1 or H4 for EURUSD or GBPUSD:

1. Name the **timeframe** and **session**.
2. Mark the **level** (swing, range edge, or round number) with a thin zone, not a one-pip line.
3. Write your **hold rule** for a real break (close, two closes, or time hold).
4. When price probes, label: `break held`, `false break`, or `unclear`.
5. If break held, watch for a **return** to the zone.
6. Label the return: `retest touch`, `retest fail` (breaks back through), or `no retest yet`.
7. If unclear, stay unclear — do not force a setup story.

Unclear is a valid educational outcome. Empty study days beat fake confidence.

## How “clean” is a retest? (soft measurement)

Beginners ask for a magic pip distance. There is no magic number that works every day on EURUSD and GBPUSD. Soft study habits instead:

- Measure how far price traveled **beyond** the level before the return (extension in pips).
- Measure how deep the return enters the zone (touch vs full reclaim).
- Note whether the retest happens in the **same session** or hours later.
- Write whether spreads were calm or news-wide.

Example notebook line (educational only):

`EURUSD | H1 | London | resistance 1.0900 | break hold close | retest -12 pips into zone | zone holds | NFA`

That line teaches more than “it retested so it must go.”

## Sessions and news change the story

Match labels to the hour:

- **Asian hours:** many EURUSD and GBPUSD days show quieter probes. A “break” on M15 can still be noise on H1. Prefer higher timeframes for break labels in quiet hours.
- **London open:** overnight levels often get tested. Wait for a few candles after the open before you lock a break-and-retest label.
- **London–New York overlap:** another active USD window. Continuations and failed retests both show up. Calendar context belongs on the same line.
- **News windows:** spikes can smash a level in one candle, then fade. Mark the event. Soft beginner approach: redraw the break/hold decision after spreads settle.

Soft study framing: do not copy a London retest label onto a sleepy Asian sample without checking the hold rule again.

## Soft study rules (not live trading rules)

Use these as classroom guardrails while you learn:

1. **Mark the zone before the break** — not after the candle looks pretty.
2. **Require a hold rule** before you say “break” — reuse false-breakout language.
3. **One timeframe for the break decision** — do not mix M5 noise into an H4 retest label without saying so.
4. **Measure extension and retest depth in pips** so samples compare across days.
5. **Separate “bounce at S/R” from “retest after break”** — different notebook words.
6. **Respect news** — treat spikes as fragile evidence for held breaks.
7. **Always NFA** — a clean retest yesterday does not guarantee continuation today.

These rules teach process. They do not tell you what to buy or sell.

## How this links to S/R, pullbacks, RR, stops, and journaling

Break and retest sits next to skills you may already study:

- **Support and resistance** supply the zones that get broken and retested.
- **False breakouts** teach the hold/fail filter you reuse before calling a break.
- **Pullback vs reversal** language helps after a break: a retest can look like a pullback in the new lean — or the start of a failed break if the zone is reclaimed.
- **Stop loss education** often sits beyond the retest zone — soft study: invalidation with room for noise, not revenge.
- **Risk-reward** sketches change after a held break: educational room toward the new lean vs back through the zone.
- **Trading journal** should store level, hold rule, extension, retest depth, hold/fail result, and final label.

Always keep the **NFA** frame: illustrative levels here are teaching props, not forecasts.

## Common beginner mistakes

**1. Calling every bounce a retest.**  
Then every touch at old highs becomes a “setup” without a prior break.

**2. Skipping the hold rule.**  
Then every wick beyond 1.0900 counts as a break.

**3. Changing the zone mid-retest.**  
That is a new plan. Label the change, or skip it in beginner samples.

**4. Mixing timeframes in one sentence.**  
“H4 break but M5 retest fail” is fine — if you write both labels.

**5. Ignoring session and news.**  
The same EURUSD retest behaves differently at 03:00 Lisbon time than at London open.

**6. Using feelings instead of zone + hold.**  
“It feels like a retest” is optional color. Break hold + return are notes.

**7. Turning a failed retest into revenge clicks.**  
Journal the miss. That is the lesson.

## How to journal break and retest in 15 minutes

No live money required. Demo charts or historical replay notes are enough.

1. Pick **EURUSD or GBPUSD** and one timeframe (H1 or H4).
2. Note **session** and any **calendar** event.
3. Mark **one clear zone** (prior swing or round number).
4. Write your **hold rule** for a real break.
5. When price probes, label break held / false break / unclear.
6. If break held, wait for a return and measure **depth in pips**.
7. Label retest result with one sentence why — not a live order.

After ten notes, look for patterns:

- Do most “breaks” you call early still fail your hold rule?
- Does GBPUSD overshoot retest zones more than EURUSD in the same London hour on your sample?
- Do news minutes create fake breaks that never get a calm retest?

Patterns beat slogans. Ten quiet lines beat one dramatic screenshot.

## Key takeaways

- A break is a move beyond a marked level that **holds** under your rule; a retest is a **return** to that zone after the hold.
- Write the zone and hold rule before the probe gets emotional.
- Reuse false-breakout discipline: vibes are not a break label.
- Measure extension and retest depth in pips, name the session, and keep one timeframe for the break decision.
- Unclear is allowed. Empty study days are valid education.
- This is educational only — not financial advice.

Keep the language concrete: “H1 close above 1.0900, then −12 pip retest that held” beats “it broke then retested perfectly.” That clarity is a beginner superpower.
